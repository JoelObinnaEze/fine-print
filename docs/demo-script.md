# Fine Print — voiceover script

Target runtime: **2:30–2:42** at a calm pace. Record one clean voice-only take; the final video will be a full-screen product recording with captions and no face camera.

## Teleprompter copy

Reward programs can fail in a subtle way: the code follows every written rule, but the rule still rewards the wrong behaviour. Fine Print tests that before launch.

Here is a referral rule. When a referred customer spends twenty dollars, both people get ten dollars in credit. The goal is to keep twenty dollars in real revenue without removing the honest reward.

Muse turns those plain-English requirements into concrete transaction tests. In this recorded run, it proposed four. Fine Print does not trust the AI's verdict: a deterministic simulator replays the transactions, removes one unnecessary step, and finds the shortest proof—refer, purchase, refund. The business keeps zero dollars while twenty dollars of reward remains spendable.

Fine Print compares repairs instead of turning the program off. Immediate reversal looks reasonable. But another Muse check spent ten dollars of credit before the purchase was refunded. Marking that reward reversed could not recover the value already spent.

That confirmed case re-ranks every repair. Fine Print recommends paying after the return window: zero observed exposure, the full twenty-dollar honest reward, and a thirty-day wait. The tradeoff is visible instead of hidden.

The same method transfers to Bottle Return. I am loading the recorded Muse case before replaying it. One bottle is deposited three times for thirty points while nothing is recycled. The three-dimensional depot makes every action and payout inspectable. The recommended rule pays once, only after an eligible item is processed.

Under the interface, Muse proposes. Strict schemas and host limits constrain every request. Deterministic simulators decide every payout and result. Confirmed cases stay attached to the exact policy version, so a later miss cannot erase earlier evidence.

The frozen twelve-case evaluation keeps every attempt, including misses and invalid sequences. Deterministic search covers the small action spaces. Muse contributes language interpretation and repair ideas; it never supplies the verdict.

Fine Print answers one practical question before launch: are we rewarding the behaviour we actually want?

## Delivery notes

- Say **Muse** as “muse,” not as individual letters.
- Pause after “zero dollars,” “value already spent,” and “nothing is recycled.”
- Read the final question more slowly than the technical section.
- Keep the tone conversational. Do not race to hit a timestamp; the edit can remove silence.
- Do not improvise stronger claims. “In the tested cases” and “bounded evidence” are deliberate limits.
